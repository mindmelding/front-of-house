# Renewal and expansion

## When this is the moment

The renewal date in row 4 is inside ninety days. Or an expansion signal appears: a question about limits or pricing tiers, a new team mentioned, a champion who got promoted, usage that crossed a plan boundary. Or the customer raised commercial terms themselves.

## What the best person on the floor does

Treats the renewal as the receipt for value already delivered, not a negotiation. Ninety days out, they can say in one sentence what the customer came for (row 8) and whether they got it, with a number. If they did, the renewal is a formality and the conversation is about what is next. If they did not, the renewal conversation is the wrong conversation; the right one is about closing the gap, and it starts now, not at the notice date.

On expansion, they reduce the friction to yes and never manufacture urgency. The seat, the team, the limit: they make it a one-line answer with the price and the path, and they let the customer's own momentum carry it.

## What an ordinary company does

Sends an automated notice at sixty days. Books a "renewal call" with a slide deck. Offers a discount for a multi-year term before anyone has asked what the product did for them.

## Steps

1. Read rows 1, 4, 5, 8. What did they come for, did it happen, and what does usage say?
2. If the desired outcome was reached: name it back to them with the evidence, in their words. Then ask what the next hundred days should do. Renewal terms come after, plainly, one sentence.
3. If it was not reached: say so first. Own the gap. Propose the path to first value with a who and a when. Do not talk terms until the gap has a plan.
4. On an expansion signal: answer the actual question (the limit, the price, the seat) in the first sentence. Offer the path in the second. Stop.
5. Anything commercial outside a standing grant (discounts, custom terms, credits against renewal) is a nudge to the operator, never an improvisation.
6. Log the renewal state and any expansion signal in the touch note.

## Guardrails specific to this moment

Never quote pricing you cannot cite from `overlay/policies.md`. Never tie a fix to a renewal ("we'll prioritize that once you renew"). Never discount unasked. Never treat a champion's promotion as a sales event before congratulating them as a person. Never pressure with a deadline you invented.

## Good / Bad example

**Bad.** "Your renewal is coming up on March 1! I'd love to hop on a call to walk through some exciting new features and discuss options for a multi-year agreement, which would lock in your current pricing."

**Good.** "You came in wanting orders to flow to the warehouse without anyone touching them. Since April that's been true 99.6% of the time (the two exceptions were the cert rotation and the Tuesday you switched carriers). Renewal is March 1 and nothing changes on our side. What should the next hundred days do for you? If the second warehouse is real, I can have seats for that team live the same day you say go."

## The 5% version

Before the renewal conversation, build the thing they mentioned once and never asked for again. Hand it over with the receipt. The renewal takes one line after that.

## Write back

Desired outcome status (reached / not / partial, with evidence), renewal date, expansion signal if any, terms discussed, any nudge raised.

## Evals

- Must state whether the desired outcome was reached, with at least one concrete fact, before any commercial term.
- Must answer an expansion question in sentence one.
- Must not quote pricing or terms absent from the snapshot's policy block.
- Must not attach a discount, deadline, or condition the customer did not raise.
