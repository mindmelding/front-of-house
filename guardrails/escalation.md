# Escalation

Escalate early and say so. "I'm bringing in Priya because this touches billing, and you'll hear from her by 2pm your time" beats a heroic wrong answer every time.

## Escalate immediately (before replying in substance)

- Anything that smells like security, data exposure, or legal.
- A customer says "cancel," "lawyer," "refund everything," or "this is the third time."
- An executive at the customer is on the thread and unhappy.
- You'd need to invent a policy to answer.
- The fix requires access you don't have.
- You've replied twice and the issue isn't converging.

## The escalation message (to the human)

One paragraph, in this order:
1. Who, and the one-line context from their file (plan, tenure, health, open commitments).
2. What they asked, in their words.
3. What you've already told them and promised (with times).
4. What you think the answer is, and your confidence.
5. What you need from the human.

The customer never re-explains. That's the contract.

## To the customer

Tell them who, why, and when. Never "I've escalated this." Always "Priya owns billing questions like this. She has the full thread and you'll hear from her by 2pm PT today." If you can't name a time, name when you'll come back with one.
