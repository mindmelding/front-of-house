# Bibliography

Every source we've drawn on, with one paragraph on what we took and one line on what we rejected or hold loosely. A bare link is not a contribution. If you add a source, add what it changed in the canon.

Links are included only where we're confident of them. Books are cited by title and author.

---

## Hospitality mindset

**Unreasonable Hospitality**, Will Guidara (2022).
What we took: the 95/5 rule (`principles/07`), one-size-fits-one (`principles/08`), the legends practice (`delight/legends/`), "intention, not perfection," and the idea that the unreasonable gesture is only possible when the routine is tight. The hot dog story is the founding parable of `delight/`.
What we hold loosely: EMP had a Dreamweaver team whose whole job was gestures. Most operators are one person. Hence the cap of three per day.

**Setting the Table**, Danny Meyer (2006).
What we took: enlightened hospitality, "collecting dots so you can connect them" (the write path of `context/CONTRACT.md`), "the road to success is paved with mistakes well handled" (`principles/19`), and the distinction between service (technical) and hospitality (how it made you feel).
What we hold loosely: the 51/49 hiring rule is about people, and an agent's "emotional 51" is a voice file. That's a stretch we're aware of.

**Four Seasons: The Story of a Business Philosophy**, Isadore Sharp (2009).
What we took: the Golden Rule as an operating system, and empowerment without a manual. "The answer is yes, now what's the question" became `principles/05`, read as "never leave someone without a path."
What we rejected: literal yes-to-everything. The nearest yes is the honest version.

**The New Gold Standard**, Joseph Michelli (2008), and the Ritz-Carlton Gold Standards (https://www.ritzcarlton.com/en/about/gold-standards).
What we took: the $2,000 per-guest empowerment rule, which became the empowerment ceiling in `guardrails/authority.md` and `principles/18`; the Mystique preference database as the ancestor of the context contract; the daily line-up as the ancestor of the weekly pre-shift in `inbox/README.md`.
What we hold loosely: the Credo card's language is too formal for our register.

**Exceptional Service, Exceptional Profit**, Leonardo Inghilleri and Micah Solomon (2010).
What we took: the chapter on language, the words a luxury operator never says. It seeded `voice/LEXICON.md` directly.
What we rejected: the scripted-greeting material. Scripts are what we're avoiding.

**Be Our Guest**, Disney Institute (2001, revised 2011).
What we took: the four keys in priority order (safety, courtesy, show, efficiency), which is the ancestor of `PRECEDENCE.md`.
What we hold loosely: "show" as a value. We'd rather the customer not notice a show at all.

**Peak**, Chip Conley (2007).
What we took: Maslow applied to guests. Meet expectations, then desires, then unrecognized needs. The third tier is where `delight/` lives.
What we hold loosely: the framework is about hotels; the mapping to software is ours.

**Hug Your Customers**, Jack Mitchell (2003), and **The Nordstrom Way**, Robert Spector and Patrick McCarthy.
What we took: retail-grade personal memory, and Nordstrom's one-rule handbook ("use good judgment in all situations") as the argument for a short mindset file over a long rulebook.
What we hold loosely: both are pre-internet retail; the frequency of contact is different.

## Engineered delight and moments

**The Power of Moments**, Chip Heath and Dan Heath (2017).
What we took: elevation, insight, pride, connection, and "break the script." `delight/PHILOSOPHY.md` is organized by it.
What we hold loosely: their examples skew toward one-time events. Ours have to survive repetition, which is why dedupe matters more here than in the book.

**Never Lose a Customer Again**, Joey Coleman (2018).
What we took: the eight phases of the first hundred days, mapped one to one in `onboarding/first-100-days.md`, and the claim that churn is decided before renewal is discussed.
What we hold loosely: the phase names. They're his; the mapping to a software product is ours.

**The Art of Gathering**, Priya Parker (2018).
What we took: a specific purpose, deciding who's in the room and why, opening with their goal, closing with owners. `onboarding/kickoff.md` is this book applied to one meeting.
What we rejected: nothing. It transfers cleanly.

**Chewy** (condolence flowers, pet portraits) and **Zappos** (the ten-hour call), as public stories.
What we took: proof that gestures travel by word of mouth. Both appear in `examples/hall-of-fame/`.
What we hold loosely: both are famous because they're rare. The Effortless Experience caveat applies.

## The counterweight

**The Effortless Experience**, Matthew Dixon, Nick Toman, and Rick DeLisi (2013).
What we took: customer effort predicts loyalty better than exceeding expectations does. Next-issue avoidance, alternative positioning, and the whole of `principles/06`. Without this book the repo becomes cupcake theater.
What we rejected: the implicit conclusion that delight is never worth it. It is, in the 5%.

## Difficult conversations

**Never Split the Difference**, Chris Voss (2016).
What we took: labeling ("sounds like this blocked the demo"), mirroring, the accusation audit, and "that's right" over "you're right." The toolkit under `principles/09` and `moments/angry-customer/`.
What we hold loosely: the hostage-negotiation framing. Customers aren't adversaries.

**Thanks for the Feedback**, Douglas Stone and Sheila Heen (2014).
What we took: how to receive criticism without arguing, which is most of `retention/exit-interview.md`.
What we rejected: nothing.

**On Apology**, Aaron Lazare (2004), and the service-recovery-paradox literature.
What we took: an apology lands on acknowledgment of the specific offense and its effect, not on the word "sorry." `principles/04` and `principles/19`.
What we hold loosely: the paradox is conditional. Fast, specific, generous, and not repeated.

**The Mom Test**, Rob Fitzpatrick (2013).
What we took: people describe solutions; ask about the job. `principles/14` and `moments/feature-request/`.
What we rejected: nothing.

## Customer success and onboarding

**Customer Success**, Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman, and Lincoln Murphy (2016), and Gainsight's ten laws.
What we took: the discipline of watching the book before the renewal. The signals table in `retention/signals.md`.
What we hold loosely: the enterprise CSM staffing model. This repo assumes one person, or one agent, per many accounts.

**Lincoln Murphy**, Sixteen Ventures (https://sixteenventures.com).
What we took: desired outcome as required outcome plus appropriate experience. Row 8 of the contract, and the day-0 question in `onboarding/time-to-first-value.md`.
What we hold loosely: nothing specific; the framing is load-bearing here.

**Product-Led Onboarding**, Ramli John (2021).
What we took: time-to-value mechanics and the idea that first value is the user's milestone, not the product's.
What we hold loosely: the self-serve bias. Our small-moments doctrine (`principles/28`) is partly a reaction to it.

**Intercom on Customer Support** and **Intercom on Onboarding** (free books from Intercom).
What we took: a working model of chat-first support and the argument that onboarding is a conversation, not a tour.
What we hold loosely: they're vendor books. Read for the practice, not the product.

**Superhuman's onboarding** (as publicly described by Rahul Vohra).
What we took: proof that a one-to-one onboarding call scales further than people assume, and the practice of asking what the user does first each morning.
What we hold loosely: it was subsidized by a premium price point.

## Support craft and voice

**Eli Weiss** (Olipop, Jones Road Beauty; writes and advises on CX).
What we took: the contemporary voice this repo is reaching for. Human, funny, generous, operationally serious. The idea that CX is the retention engine in direct-to-consumer, and that the transactional interactions are where it's built.
What we hold loosely: the DTC contexts are higher volume and lower price point than most B2B software.

**The Customer Support Handbook**, Sarah Hatter (2014).
What we took: support as a craft with standards, and the insistence on writing like a person.
What we hold loosely: some tooling references have aged.

**Help Scout blog** (https://www.helpscout.com/blog/), including Mat Patterson's writing on support language.
What we took: the phrases-to-avoid and phrases-to-use pairs that seeded the substitutes table in `voice/LEXICON.md`.
What we hold loosely: a few of their "phrases to use" have since become tells themselves.

**Support Driven** (https://www.supportdriven.com).
What we took: a community of practitioners whose threads are a better source of new situations than any book. A standing input to `inbox/`.
What we hold loosely: opinions vary; triage before promoting.

**Public voice guides.** Mailchimp Content Style Guide (https://styleguide.mailchimp.com), Monzo tone of voice (https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice), Atlassian Design System content guidelines (https://atlassian.design/content), Shopify Polaris content guidelines (https://polaris.shopify.com/content), GOV.UK style guide (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/style-guide), 18F content guide (https://guides.18f.gov/content-guide/).
What we took: specify behavior, not adjectives. Per-channel registers. Monzo's tone-varies-by-moment table is the ancestor of `voice/channels/`.
What we hold loosely: each is tuned for its brand. We took the structure, not the words.

**Public support handbooks.** GitLab (https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/support/), PostHog (https://posthog.com/handbook), Basecamp (https://github.com/basecamp/handbook), Automattic's Happiness Engineers, Wistia.
What we took: the practice of writing the whole operating model down in public, and the proof that it makes the writing better.
What we hold loosely: GitLab's is enormous. Ours has to stay loadable.

**Nicely Said**, Nicole Fenton and Kate Kiefer Lee (2014).
What we took: writing for people, sentence by sentence. Read it before editing `voice/VOICE.md`.
What we rejected: nothing.

**Bobby Stuckey** (Frasca Food and Wine), talks on hospitality versus service.
What we took: service is technical, hospitality is how it made you feel. The clearest statement of the distinction that runs through `MINDSET.md`.
What we hold loosely: restaurant-specific examples.

## Agent-side

**Building effective agents**, Anthropic (https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents), and **Effective context engineering for AI agents** (https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents).
What we took: keep the always-loaded context small; load the rest on demand. The reason `MINDSET.md` is under 800 words and the moments are separate skills.
What we hold loosely: nothing; the constraints are real.

**Agent Skills** (https://agentskills.io), **AGENTS.md** (https://agents.md), **Cursor rules** (https://docs.cursor.com/context/rules), **llms.txt** (https://llmstxt.org).
What we took: the formats the adapters generate. Skills as the native unit (`decisions/2026-09-14-agent-skills-as-native-unit.md`).
What we hold loosely: these conventions are young. Adapters exist so the canon outlives any of them.

**Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).
What we took: the catalog in `voice/ai-tells.md`. The best public source on the default register.
What we hold loosely: the order shifts about once a year; we refresh monthly.

**Every's AI style guide** (https://every.to/guides/ai-style-guide).
What we took: voice defined as tensions, paired good/bad examples, and building a guide by interviewing the writer against alternatives. `voice/VOICE.md` follows it.
What we hold loosely: their register is editorial; ours is a colleague in Slack.

**Sierra, Intercom Fin, Decagon** (their public writing on agent guidance, agent operating procedures, and development lifecycles).
What we took: prior art for opinions as agent configuration, and for evals as the thing that makes guidance real.
What we hold loosely: vendor framing. We took the shape, not the products.

## Cautionary tales

Each one is in `examples/hall-of-shame/` and has, or will have, an eval case.

**Cursor's support agent, April 2025.** An AI support agent told users that a one-device login limit was policy. It wasn't; a session bug was the cause. Users cancelled publicly before a founder corrected it.
What we took: `principles/11`, never invent policy. If it isn't written, escalate.

**Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149 (February 2024).** A tribunal held the airline liable for a bereavement-fare refund its chatbot had described incorrectly. The airline's argument that the chatbot was a separate entity was rejected.
What we took: the agent's words are the company's words. Same principle.

**Chevrolet dealership chatbot, December 2023.** A dealer's chatbot was prompted into agreeing to sell a vehicle for one dollar and calling it a legally binding offer.
What we took: never make a commitment with cost outside a grant (`guardrails/authority.md`), and treat playful prompts as seriously as sincere ones.

**Klarna, 2025.** After publicly replacing much of its support staff with an AI assistant, the company said quality had suffered and began hiring humans back.
What we took: the small-moments doctrine (`principles/28`) and the handoff standard (`principles/25`). Cost-first automation removes the moments that build the relationship.
What we hold loosely: the details of what was automated and what was reversed are the company's account of its own decisions.
