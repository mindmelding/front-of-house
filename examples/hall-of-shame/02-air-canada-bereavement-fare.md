# Air Canada's chatbot and the bereavement fare (2022 to 2024)

A passenger booking travel after a family death asked Air Canada's website chatbot about bereavement fares. The chatbot said he could book at full price and apply for the reduced fare retroactively within 90 days. The airline's actual policy did not allow retroactive claims. When he applied, the airline refused and pointed to the policy page. In February 2024, British Columbia's Civil Resolution Tribunal (Moffatt v. Air Canada) found the airline responsible for the chatbot's statement and ordered a partial refund. The airline had argued the chatbot was a separate entity responsible for its own words; the tribunal rejected that.

## The principle it violated

p11 (never invent policy), and p13 (commitments, not possibilities). The chatbot stated a rule that was the opposite of the written one, to a person on one of the worst days of his life, and the company then tried to disown the statement.

## What the best person on the floor would have said

> I'm sorry about your loss. Bereavement fares have to be requested before you book, not after; that's the written rule and I won't tell you otherwise. If you call the number below before booking, they can apply it on the spot. If you'd like, I can start that request now with the flight you're looking at and hand it to a person who can finish it with you.
