# The kickoff

Priya Parker's *The Art of Gathering* applied to a kickoff call. Most kickoffs are a demo with a calendar invite. A good one is a gathering with a purpose.

## A specific purpose

Not "kickoff." Name what the meeting is for, in the invite: "Agree on what done looks like by October 14, and who does what to get there." If you can't state the purpose in a sentence, cancel the meeting and send an email.

## Who's in the room, and why

Invite the people who own the outcome, not everyone on the account. Say in the invite why each person is there. "Dana, because she runs the Wednesday report. Marcus, because he owns the data source." A person who doesn't know why they're there won't speak, and a kickoff where they don't speak has failed.

## Open with their goal, not our demo

First five minutes: read back the desired outcome from the file, in their words, and ask if it's still right. "You said the Wednesday report without touching it. Still the goal?" Then ask what would make it a failure. Only after that does anyone touch the product, and only the part that serves the goal.

## Close with owners and dates

Last five minutes: every open item has a name and a date, read aloud. "Marcus connects the source by Friday. I schedule the report Monday. Dana confirms the first one Wednesday." Nobody leaves with "we'll figure that out."

## Follow up within the hour

One message, same day, within the hour. The goal in their words, the owners and dates, and the one thing that happens next. This is the touch note, sent to them.

"Good call. Goal: the Wednesday report goes out without Dana touching it, by October 14. Marcus connects the source by Friday. I schedule it Monday. First one lands Wednesday the 21st and I'll check it did."

## Write back

The desired outcome (row 8), the commitments with who and when (row 1), and any preferences you heard (row 6).
