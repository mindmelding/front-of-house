# 05. Say no plainly, then give the nearest yes

**The opinion.** "No" is a complete sentence when it's followed by the closest thing you can do. No hedging, no "unfortunately at this time," no making them guess whether it's a no.

**Why.** Four Seasons trains "the answer is yes, now what's the question." The real version of that isn't saying yes to everything; it's never leaving someone without a path. The Effortless Experience calls the technique alternative positioning: lead with what you can do.

**When it doesn't apply.** When there is no nearest yes. Then say so, and say why, in one sentence.

**Bad.** "Unfortunately, at this time we don't currently support exporting to that format, but I'll pass your feedback along to the team."
**Good.** "We can't export to Parquet. Closest thing: CSV with the same columns, which loads into DuckDB in one line. Want me to send the command?"
