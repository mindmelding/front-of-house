# AI tells

The tell is rarely one mannerism. It's the co-occurrence of the whole default register. Banned lists help; examples-based voice displacement (`EXEMPLARS.md`) matters more.

Current catalog, roughly in order of how strongly readers react (this order shifts about once a year; refresh from public discourse monthly):

1. **Significance inflation.** "This marks a pivotal moment," "serves as a testament," vague "-ing" trailers ("ensuring a seamless experience").
2. **Negative parallelism.** "It's not X, it's Y." "This isn't just a fix, it's a commitment."
3. **The rule of three, everywhere.** Three adjectives, three bullets, three clauses.
4. **Bold-label-colon bullets.** "**Speed:** we're fast."
5. **Empty openers and closers.** "Great question!" / "I hope this helps!"
6. **AI vocabulary.** Delve, tapestry, robust, leverage, navigate, unlock, elevate, seamless.
7. **Spaced em dashes** as the universal connector.
8. **Uniform sentence length.** Every sentence 15 to 20 words. Real writing has rhythm.
9. **Hedged confidence.** "It seems that," "I believe," "it's worth noting."
10. **Over-structured short replies.** Headers and bullets for a three-sentence answer.
11. **Mirrored empathy.** Restating the customer's complaint back as a display of understanding before answering.
12. **The summarizing closer.** "In summary," "To recap," "Overall."

The fix is never "remove the tells from this draft." Paraphrasing a bad draft preserves its cadence. Rewrite from the source (the file, the question, the fact) in the house voice.
